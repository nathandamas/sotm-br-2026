# Results-ready synthesis

The intrinsic OSM typology comprised three operational classes across 895 1-km cells. A structural class contained 114 cells (12.7%) with no currently mapped OSM highway-line geometry. Among the 781 road-present cells, 718 were assigned to a lower-intensity, longer-plateau mapping regime and 63 to a higher-intensity, expansion-pulse mapping regime. The higher-intensity regime had a median mapped-road length of 4.75 km versus 2.27 km, a median of 96 versus 28 contributions, and a higher active-month ratio (0.0625 versus 0.0402). Its road-history profile was distinguished most strongly by an initial growth rate near 95.2 m/month versus 0.0, a growth-deceleration metric near 93.8 m/month versus 0.0, more positive-growth months (7 versus 4), and a larger peak growth event (about 2.14 km versus 1.40 km). By contrast, the lower-intensity regime showed a longer median plateau duration (70 versus 59 months) and more months since first reaching 90% of its recent intrinsic road-length reference (75 versus 68 months). These labels therefore summarize relative mapping-history regimes only and must not be interpreted as external completeness, positional accuracy, or real-world road availability.

The typology was spatially structured: 0.743 of Queen-neighbor edges connected cells of the same class, compared with a permutation expectation of 0.664 (p=0.001; 999 permutations). Spatial coherence was evaluated descriptively and was not imposed as a clustering constraint.
